## 1. break-even-calculator-excel-google-sheets

Title candidates
- How to Build a Break-Even Calculator in Excel and Google Sheets (**Chosen**: exact search phrase, both apps, action "build")
- Break-Even Calculator for a Farmers Market Booth in Excel (niche example, but drops Google Sheets)
- How to Calculate Break-Even Units in Excel and Google Sheets (matches "break even units formula" searches)

First paragraph candidates
- A. (**Chosen**) Divide your fixed costs by the profit per unit and round up: `=ROUNDUP(D2/E2,0)`, where E2 is price minus variable cost. A $120.00 booth fee and an $11.50 profit per candle means you need to sell 11 candles to break even.
  - Why: gives the exact formula and a concrete booth example in two sentences, and ROUNDUP answers the "do I round up or down" question.
- B. Use `=ROUNDUP(FixedCosts/(Price-VariableCost),0)` to find how many units cover your costs, then multiply by the price for break-even revenue.
  - Why: matches the generic formula searchers type, but has no example.
- C. Break-even units equal fixed costs divided by profit per unit, always rounded up. In Excel or Google Sheets that is one formula per product.
  - Why: states the concept, but the exact formula is missing, so it is weaker as an answer-first paragraph.

## 2. subscription-renewal-tracker-excel-google-sheets

Title candidates
- How to Track Subscription Renewals in Excel and Google Sheets (**Chosen**: "track" plus both apps, close to "subscription tracker spreadsheet")
- Subscription Renewal Tracker in Google Sheets (short, drops Excel)
- How to Calculate the Next Renewal Date in Excel and Google Sheets (matches the EDATE search)

First paragraph candidates
- A. (**Chosen**) Find each renewal date with `=EDATE(B2,C2)`, which adds whole months to the last charge date, then count the days left with `=E2-TODAY()`. Add an IF on the days left to flag anything due within 30 days.
  - Why: both formulas and the 30-day flag in two sentences; EDATE is what searchers for "next renewal date formula" need.
- B. Add months to a date with `=EDATE(B2,C2)`. It handles month-end dates for you, so 08/31/2026 plus 6 months returns 02/28/2027.
  - Why: answers the month-end worry directly, but skips the days-left and flag part of the template.
- C. Type the last charge date and the billing cycle in months, and `=EDATE(B2,C2)` returns the next renewal.
  - Why: plain and short, but has no example value and no flag.

## 3. budget-vs-actual-variance-excel-google-sheets

Title candidates
- How to Create a Budget vs Actual Template in Excel and Google Sheets (**Chosen**: matches "budget vs actual template excel")
- Budget vs Actual Variance Formula in Excel and Google Sheets (matches formula searches)
- How to Calculate Variance Between Budget and Actual in Excel (drops Google Sheets)

First paragraph candidates
- A. (**Chosen**) Subtract budget from actual with `=C2-B2` for the dollar variance, and use `=IF(B2=0,"",(C2-B2)/B2)` for the percent. The IF leaves the percent blank when the budget is $0.00 instead of showing a divide-by-zero error.
  - Why: gives both formulas and the zero-budget case that trips people up.
- B. Variance is actual minus budget: `=C2-B2`. A positive number means you spent more than planned.
  - Why: answers the sign question, but leaves out the percent formula.
- C. Put Budget in column B and Actual in column C, then `=C2-B2` gives the variance for each category.
  - Why: easy to follow, but has no percent and no red-flag step.

## 4. farmers-market-sales-log-excel-google-sheets

Title candidates
- How to Track Farmers Market Sales in Excel and Google Sheets (**Chosen**: "track", both apps, matches "farmers market sales tracker spreadsheet")
- Farmers Market Sales Log With SUMPRODUCT in Excel (names the formula, drops Google Sheets)
- How to Total Daily Booth Sales in Excel and Google Sheets (matches the day-total search)

First paragraph candidates
- A. (**Chosen**) Multiply quantity by price and add it all up in one step with `=SUMPRODUCT(C2:C20,D2:D20)`. To get one market day's revenue, wrap the dates in the formula, as in `=SUMPRODUCT((A2:A20=F2)*C2:C20*D2:D20)`.
  - Why: both the all-days and per-day formulas in two sentences, and the reader needs no helper column.
- B. Log each item you sell as a row, then `=SUMIFS(E2:E20,A2:A20,F2)` totals one market day.
  - Why: simple, but SUMIFS overlaps the Etsy SUMIFS post; SUMPRODUCT is the angle for this one.
- C. SUMPRODUCT multiplies two columns row by row and adds the results, so one formula gives your booth revenue without a line-total column.
  - Why: explains the function, but no formula with real cell references.

## 5. clean-customer-list-trim-proper-excel-google-sheets

Title candidates
- How to Clean Up a Customer List With TRIM and PROPER in Excel and Google Sheets (**Chosen**: names the functions people search, both apps, action "clean up")
- How to Fix Names in ALL CAPS and Extra Spaces in Excel (matches the symptom searches)
- How to Clean Up Names in Excel and Google Sheets (short and close to "clean up names excel")

First paragraph candidates
- A. (**Chosen**) Type `=PROPER(TRIM(CLEAN(A2)))` next to the messy name and copy it down. It removes extra spaces, turns ALL CAPS into Proper Case, and strips most hidden characters.
  - Why: the exact formula first, plus the three problems people search for. Non-breaking spaces and line breaks are in the body, because this formula does not fix them (checked in LibreOffice Calc).
- B. TRIM removes extra spaces, PROPER fixes capitalization, and CLEAN deletes hidden characters. Nest them as `=PROPER(TRIM(CLEAN(A2)))`.
  - Why: explains each function, but the formula comes second.
- C. Copied names often keep spaces that TRIM cannot remove, such as non-breaking spaces, so clean them in two steps.
  - Why: a good troubleshooting angle, but not an answer-first paragraph, so it goes in the body.
