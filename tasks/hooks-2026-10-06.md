  - Why: exact formula plus a worked number the template shows, and the minus sign answers the "why is it negative" search.

  - Why: speaks to the usual mistake, but the full formula is missing.

First paragraph candidates
- A. (**Chosen**) Subtract last month from this month and divide by last month: `=IF(B2=0,"",(C2-B2)/B2)`, then format the cell as Percent. Going from $2,400.00 to $2,760.00 shows 15.0%.
  - Why: formula, format step and a checkable example in two sentences, with the zero-month case covered.

- C. Divide the change by the earlier month, not the later one, or the percent will be off.
  - Why: names the common mistake, but gives no exact formula.

  - Why: promises the result but lacks the formula.

## 5. combine-address-columns-textjoin-excel-google-sheets

Title candidates
- How to Combine Address Columns With TEXTJOIN in Excel and Google Sheets (**Chosen**: names the function, both apps, action "combine")
- How to Join Cells and Skip Blanks in Excel and Google Sheets (matches the "skip blanks" search)
- How to Make a Mailing Address Line in Excel and Google Sheets (matches the label use)

First paragraph candidates
- A. (**Chosen**) Type `=TEXTJOIN(", ",TRUE,A2:D2)` to join street, unit, city and state into one line. The TRUE skips empty cells, so a row with no unit does not leave a stray comma.
  - Why: exact formula and the blank-skipping benefit, which is what the search is about.
- B. TEXTJOIN takes a separator, an ignore-empty setting and a range, so one formula replaces a chain of & joins.
  - Why: explains the syntax, but no example formula.
- C. The ampersand leaves extra commas when a cell is empty, and TEXTJOIN does not.
  - Why: states the contrast the template shows, but is weaker without the formula.
