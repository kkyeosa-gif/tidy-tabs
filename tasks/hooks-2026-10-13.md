## 1. invoice-due-date-payment-terms-excel-google-sheets

Title candidates
- How to Calculate an Invoice Due Date From Payment Terms in Excel and Google Sheets (**Chosen**: action "calculate", both apps, matches "due date from terms")
- How to Add Net 30 Days to an Invoice Date in Excel and Google Sheets (matches the Net 30 search but covers one term only)
- How to Show Days Until an Invoice Is Due in Excel and Google Sheets (matches the countdown search, a secondary result of the template)

First paragraph candidates
- A. (**Chosen**) Add the days in the terms to the invoice date with `=C2+VLOOKUP(D2,$J$2:$K$6,2,FALSE)`, where C2 is the invoice date and J2:K6 lists each terms name next to its number of days. An invoice dated 09/01/2026 on Net 30 terms is due 10/01/2026.
  - Why: exact formula, the layout it assumes, and a checkable example.
- B. For Net 30 terms, type `=C2+30`. Dates are numbers, so adding 30 gives the due date.
  - Why: the quickest answer, but it hardcodes one term and the template uses a lookup.
- C. Subtract today from the due date with `=F2-TODAY()` to see how many days are left.
  - Why: answers the countdown search, but needs the due date first.

## 3. repeat-customer-rate-countif-excel-google-sheets

First paragraph candidates
- A. (**Chosen**) Count orders per customer with `=COUNTIF(Orders!$B$2:$B$200,A2)`, then divide repeat customers by customers who ordered with `=COUNTIF(B2:B9,">=2")/COUNTIF(B2:B9,">0")`. In the sample, 3 of 7 customers ordered more than once, a 42.9% repeat rate.
  - Why: both formulas and a checkable result in two sentences.
- B. A repeat customer is anyone with two or more orders, so count them with `=COUNTIF(B2:B9,">=2")`.
  - Why: defines the term, but gives only the numerator.
- C. Put each customer once on a list and count their orders beside the name.
  - Why: describes the layout with no formula.

  - Why: describes the idea with no formula.

  - Why: a good answer for the prefix search, but it undersells the three-part template.
