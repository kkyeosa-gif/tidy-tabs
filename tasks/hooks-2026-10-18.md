## 1. lookup-with-two-criteria-index-match-sumifs-excel-google-sheets

Titles
- A. How to Look Up a Value With Two Criteria in Excel and Google Sheets. Closest to "vlookup with two criteria excel" and names both apps.
- B. How to Use INDEX MATCH With Two Conditions in Excel and Google Sheets. Matches "index match multiple criteria" for people who already know the function pair.
- C. How to Find a Price by Size and Finish in Excel and Google Sheets. Matches the "lookup value based on two columns" intent with the shop example, but narrower in wording.

First paragraphs
- A. Join the two lookup columns into a helper key, then match on that key with `=INDEX(Prices!C2:C10,MATCH(B1&"|"&B2,Prices!D2:D10,0))`. The helper in D2 is `=A2&"|"&B2`, filled down. Reason: gives the whole recipe in two sentences, works in every app, no array entry.
- B. When one column is not enough to find the row, use `=SUMIFS(Prices!C2:C10,Prices!A2:A10,B1,Prices!B2:B10,B2)` for a number. It returns 0 instead of an error when nothing matches. Reason: shortest working answer for a number lookup, but hides the INDEX MATCH wording people search.
- C. To find a price by size and finish, match on both at once with `=INDEX(Prices!C2:C10,MATCH(B1&"|"&B2,Prices!D2:D10,0))`. This returns the price for the row where both the size and the finish agree. Reason: uses the searcher's own example, but repeats itself and skips the helper key explanation.

CHOSEN
title: How to Look Up a Value With Two Criteria in Excel and Google Sheets

opening: Join the two lookup columns into a helper key, then match on that key with `=INDEX(Prices!C2:C10,MATCH(B1&"|"&B2,Prices!D2:D10,0))`. The helper in D2 is `=A2&"|"&B2`, filled down the price list.

search_description: Look up a value with two criteria using =INDEX(Prices!C2:C10,MATCH(B1&"|"&B2,Prices!D2:D10,0)) in Excel and Google Sheets. Free price list template.

## 2. stock-on-hand-from-in-out-log-sumifs-excel-google-sheets

- B. How to Calculate Current Inventory From Purchases and Sales With SUMIFS in Excel and Google Sheets. Matches "calculate stock on hand excel sumif" but is long and says purchases, which the log calls Received.
- C. How to Build a Simple Stock Ledger in Excel and Google Sheets. Matches "simple stock ledger template google sheets", shortest, but "ledger" is less common than "inventory" for small sellers.

First paragraphs
- A. Keep one log of every receipt and sale, then total each SKU with `=SUMIFS(Log!D:D,Log!B:B,A2,Log!C:C,"Received")-SUMIFS(Log!D:D,Log!B:B,A2,Log!C:C,"Sold")`. Add a third SUMIFS for signed Adjustment rows to cover counts and breakage. Reason: formula first, and the adjustment note matches what the template does.

Use `=SUMIFS(Log!D:D,Log!B:B,A2,Log!C:C,"Received")-SUMIFS(Log!D:D,Log!B:B,A2,Log!C:C,"Sold")` and fill it down the SKU list. Reason: explains the idea before the formula, so the formula lands one sentence late.
- C. A stock ledger is a log where each row is one movement, and the current count is a SUMIFS over that log. Start with `=SUMIFS(Log!D:D,Log!B:B,A2,Log!C:C,"Received")-SUMIFS(Log!D:D,Log!B:B,A2,Log!C:C,"Sold")`. Reason: introduces the ledger term for people who searched it, at the cost of delaying the answer.

## 3. extract-domain-from-email-address-left-mid-find-excel-google-sheets

First paragraphs
- A. Find the @ sign, then take the text after it with `=MID(A2,FIND("@",A2)+1,LEN(A2))`. The username is the text before it, `=LEFT(A2,FIND("@",A2)-1)`. Reason: both formulas in two sentences for both searcher goals.
- B. Splitting at the @ sign takes one FIND and two functions: `=LEFT(A2,FIND("@",A2)-1)` returns the username and `=MID(A2,FIND("@",A2)+1,LEN(A2))` returns the domain. Reason: explains the split logic matching the title, a single sentence with both answers.

It checks shape only: one @, a dot after it and no spaces. Reason: direct answer to the format check query, but the formula is long for an opening and does not give the domain.

CHOSEN
title: How to Get the Domain or Username From an Email Address in Excel and Google Sheets

opening: Find the @ sign, then take everything after it with `=MID(A2,FIND("@",A2)+1,LEN(A2))`. The username is everything before it, `=LEFT(A2,FIND("@",A2)-1)`.

## 4. on-time-shipping-rate-promised-vs-shipped-date-excel-google-sheets

Titles
- A. How to Calculate Your On Time Shipping Rate in Excel and Google Sheets. Matches "on time shipping rate formula excel" and says calculate.
- B. How to Find Late Orders by Comparing Ship By and Shipped Dates in Excel and Google Sheets. Matches "compare two dates excel late", no hyphen in "ship by".
- C. How to Count Orders Shipped Late in Excel and Google Sheets. Matches "count late orders excel", short and plain.

First paragraphs
- A. Mark each shipped order On time when the shipped date is on or before the ship by date, then divide: `=COUNTIF(F2:F31,"On time")/COUNT(D2:D31)`. Orders without a shipped date are left out of both counts. Reason: gives the rate formula first, and the exclusion rule that otherwise skews the rate.
- B. Compare the two dates with `=IF(D2<=C2,"On time","Late")`, where C2 is the ship by date and D2 is the shipped date. A shipped date on the ship by day counts as on time. Reason: answers the date comparison query but stops short of the rate.
- C. Count late orders with `=COUNTIF(F2:F31,"Late")`, after marking each row with `=IF(D2<=C2,"On time","Late")`. Reason: matches the count query, but the opening gives a count rather than the rate in the title.

CHOSEN
title: How to Calculate Your On Time Shipping Rate in Excel and Google Sheets

opening: Mark each shipped order On time when the shipped date is on or before the ship by date, then divide the on time count by the shipped count with `=COUNTIF(F2:F31,"On time")/COUNT(D2:D31)`. Orders with no shipped date are left out of both counts.

search_description: Calculate an on time shipping rate with =COUNTIF(F2:F31,"On time")/COUNT(D2:D31) in Excel and Google Sheets. Free order tracker template.

- A. Multiply width by height in inches, divide by 144 for square feet, then apply the rate and keep the larger of that and your minimum: `=MAX($F$1,ROUND(B5*C5/144*D5*$F$2,2))`.
