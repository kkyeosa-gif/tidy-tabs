## 2. sum-same-cell-across-monthly-sheets-3d-sum-indirect-excel-google-sheets

Titles
- A. How to Add the Same Cell Across Monthly Sheets in Excel and Google Sheets (matches "sum same cell across sheets")
- B. How to Sum Across Multiple Sheets in Excel and Google Sheets (shorter and broader, higher chance of a match)
- C. How to Roll Up Monthly Tabs Into a Year Total in Excel and Google Sheets (speaks to the bookkeeping habit, fewer exact matches)

Openings
- A. In Excel, `=SUM(Jan:Apr!B2)` adds cell B2 from every sheet between Jan and Apr. Google Sheets has no 3D reference, so use `=Jan!B2+Feb!B2+Mar!B2+Apr!B2` there. (gives both apps in two sentences)
- B. Add cell B2 from each monthly tab with `=Jan!B2+Feb!B2+Mar!B2+Apr!B2`, which works in both apps. Excel can shorten it to `=SUM(Jan:Apr!B2)`. (leads with the version that works everywhere)
- C. Type the tab names in a column and pull each value with `=INDIRECT("'"&A9&"'!B2")`, then sum the column. (leads with the flexible method, less direct)

CHOSEN
title: How to Add the Same Cell Across Monthly Sheets in Excel and Google Sheets

opening: Add cell B2 from each monthly tab with `=Jan!B2+Feb!B2+Mar!B2+Apr!B2`, which works in both apps. Excel can shorten it to `=SUM(Jan:Apr!B2)`, but Google Sheets does not support that 3D reference.

search_description: Add the same cell across monthly sheets with =Jan!B2+Feb!B2+Mar!B2+Apr!B2 or =SUM(Jan:Apr!B2) in Excel and Google Sheets. Free monthly rollup template.

## 3. find-outlier-order-amounts-quartile-iqr-excel-google-sheets

Titles
- A. How to Find Unusually High or Low Order Amounts in Excel and Google Sheets (plain wording for a shop owner)
- B. How to Spot Typos and Outliers in a Sales Column in Excel and Google Sheets (names the real problem, a typed extra zero)
- C. How to Flag Outliers With QUARTILE and the IQR Rule in Excel and Google Sheets (exact for statistics searchers, jargon for others)

Openings
- A. Flag an amount as unusual when it is above `=QUARTILE(C2:C31,3)+1.5*(QUARTILE(C2:C31,3)-QUARTILE(C2:C31,1))` or below the matching lower fence with Q1 minus 1.5 times the same spread. (gives the full rule)
- B. Compute Q1 and Q3 with QUARTILE, take the difference as the IQR, and review any amount more than 1.5 IQRs outside them. (explains the steps in order)
- C. A mean plus 2 standard deviations rule often misses a typo because the typo inflates the standard deviation, so use quartile fences instead. (explains why, then points to the method)

CHOSEN
title: How to Find Unusually High or Low Order Amounts in Excel and Google Sheets

opening: Flag an amount for review when it is above Q3 + 1.5 x IQR or below Q1 - 1.5 x IQR, where `=QUARTILE(C2:C31,1)` and `=QUARTILE(C2:C31,3)` give Q1 and Q3 and IQR is their difference. A typed $500.00 among orders of $35.00 to $75.00 lands outside the fences, while a mean and standard deviation rule can miss it.

search_description: Find unusually high or low order amounts with QUARTILE and the 1.5 x IQR rule in Excel and Google Sheets. Free outlier checker for a sales column.

## 4. return-on-ad-spend-roas-break-even-excel-google-sheets

Titles
- A. How to Calculate Return on Ad Spend (ROAS) and Break Even ROAS in Excel and Google Sheets (both searches in one title)
- B. How to Calculate Ad Cost Per Sale and Profit After Ads in Excel and Google Sheets (matches sellers who think in profit)
- C. How to Tell If Etsy or Facebook Ads Are Paying Off in Excel and Google Sheets (conversational, no formula term for matching)

Openings
- A. ROAS is sales divided by ad spend, so `=D5/B5` gives it for each campaign. Your break even ROAS is 1 divided by your gross margin, such as `=1/B1`. (formula first, both numbers)
- B. Compare each campaign's ROAS with `=1/B1`, the break even ROAS for your gross margin, and read profit after ads as `=D5*$B$1-B5`. (leads with the decision rule)
- C. A 40% margin needs a ROAS of 2.50 just to break even, because every $1.00 of ads must bring back $2.50 in sales. (a concrete number first, less formula)

CHOSEN
title: How to Calculate Return on Ad Spend (ROAS) and Break Even ROAS in Excel and Google Sheets

opening: ROAS is sales divided by ad spend, so `=D5/B5` gives it for each campaign. Break even ROAS is 1 divided by your gross margin: with a 40% margin, `=1/B1` returns 2.50.

search_description: Calculate ROAS with =D5/B5 and break even ROAS with =1/B1 in Excel and Google Sheets. Free ad spend template with profit after ads.

- C. A Pareto table needs only two things: revenue sorted largest first and a running share of the total. (plain summary, no formula)

CHOSEN
title: How to Find the Products That Make Up 80 Percent of Your Sales in Excel and Google Sheets

opening: Sort products by revenue, largest first, then use `=SUM($B$2:B2)/SUM($B$2:$B$16)` for the cumulative share of sales. The rows up to the one that first reaches 80% are your top group.

search_description: Find the products that make up 80 percent of sales with a cumulative percent formula in Excel and Google Sheets. Free Pareto 80/20 template, no chart needed.
