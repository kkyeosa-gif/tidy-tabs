## 1. average-annual-sales-growth-cagr-rri-excel-google-sheets

Titles
- A. How to Calculate Average Yearly Sales Growth (CAGR) in Excel and Google Sheets (plain question plus the term people search)
- B. How to Use RRI to Find the Compound Annual Growth Rate in Excel and Google Sheets (catches searchers who already know the function)
- C. How to Calculate How Fast Your Shop Sales Grew Per Year in Excel and Google Sheets (shop owner wording, fewer exact matches)

Dropped: How to Calculate CAGR in Excel and Google Sheets (too short, no sales angle); How to Find Average Annual Growth Rate in Excel and Google Sheets (no CAGR term); How to Use the RRI Function in Excel and Google Sheets (function help page territory, no small business angle)

Openings
- A. Find the average yearly growth rate with `=RRI(4,B2,B6)`, where 4 is the number of years between the first and last sales figure, B2 is the first year's sales and B6 is the last year's. (answers at once with the exact formula; plan addresses B2 and B6)
- B. CAGR is `=(end/start)^(1/periods)-1`, and RRI does the same in one step: `=RRI(periods,start,end)`. Count periods as the last year minus the first year, not the number of years listed. (gives both forms and the usual counting mistake)
- C. One average rate hides uneven years, so work out the yearly rate with `=RRI(E2-E1,start,end)` and then check each year against it. (leads with the decision, less direct; plan addresses E1 and E2)

CHOSEN
title: How to Calculate Average Yearly Sales Growth (CAGR) in Excel and Google Sheets

opening: Find the average yearly growth rate with `=RRI(4,B2,B6)`, where 4 is the number of years between the first and last sales figure, B2 is the first year's sales and B6 is the last year's. The same result comes from `=(B6/B2)^(1/4)-1`.

search_description: Calculate average yearly sales growth (CAGR) with =RRI(periods,start,end) in Excel and Google Sheets. Free growth rate template with a year by year table.

Flags for script-writer
- PLAN addresses: B2 (2022 sales) and B6 (2026 sales), year inputs E1 and E2. Confirm in the builder. If the template computes periods in a cell (`=E2-E1`), the post may show that instead of the typed 4.
- The topics file's spot check `RRI(3,12000,21500)` = 21.456% used 3 periods. The planned data (2022 to 2026) has 4 periods, so do NOT quote 21.456% for this sample. Take the real result from the built template.
- Google Sheets has RRI; link Google's help page, do not claim a Sheets test.
