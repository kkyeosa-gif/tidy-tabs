## 1. orders-by-state-countifs-sumifs-excel-google-sheets
Title candidates: A. How to Count Orders and Sales by State in Excel and Google Sheets (chosen, names the job and both apps). B. Sales by State Tracker for Etsy Orders in Google Sheets (drops Excel). C. How to Total Orders by Ship-To State in Excel (drops Sheets).
Chosen opening: Count the orders for a state with `=COUNTIFS(Orders!$C$5:$C$304,A5)` and add up its sales with `=SUMIFS(Orders!$D$5:$D$304,Orders!$C$5:$C$304,A5)`, where A5 holds the state code such as MA. In the sample, MA has 6 orders totaling $234.00.

## 2. order-status-counts-oldest-open-order-countif-excel-google-sheets
Title: How to Count Orders by Status and Find the Oldest Open Order in Excel and Google Sheets.
Chosen opening: Count the orders in one status with `=COUNTIF(Orders!$C$6:$C$205,A5)`, then name the oldest open order with `=INDEX(Orders!A6:A205,MATCH(MAX(Orders!E6:E205),Orders!E6:E205,0))`. In the sample, order 3103 has been open 20 days as of 11/04/2026.

## 3. count-words-in-cell-product-description-len-substitute-excel-google-sheets
Title: How to Count Words in a Cell in Excel and Google Sheets.
Chosen opening: Count the words in B6 with `=LEN(TRIM(B6))-LEN(SUBSTITUTE(TRIM(B6)," ",""))+1`, which is the number of spaces between words plus 1. An empty cell still returns 1, so the template wraps this in an IF that returns 0.

## 4. clearance-price-minimum-margin-floor-max-roundup-excel-google-sheets
Title: How to Set Clearance Prices With a Minimum Margin in Excel and Google Sheets.
Chosen opening: Take the marked-down price in F6 but never go below the floor with `=MIN(C6,MAX(F6,G6))`, where the floor is `=ROUNDUP(B6/(1-$B$2),2)`. A $10.20 item marked down 50% from $22.00 stays at $12.75 with a 20.0% minimum margin.

## 5. pieces-per-letter-sheet-int-roundup-excel-google-sheets
Title: How to Calculate How Many Pieces Fit on a US Letter Sheet in Excel and Google Sheets.
Chosen opening: Multiply pieces across by pieces down with `=INT(ROUND(($B$2-2*$B$4+D8)/(B8+D8),6))*INT(ROUND(($B$3-2*$B$4+D8)/(C8+D8),6))`, then divide the quantity by that with `=ROUNDUP(E8/H8,0)`. Turned sideways, 3.5 x 2 in business cards fit 12 per sheet, so 250 cards need 21 sheets.
