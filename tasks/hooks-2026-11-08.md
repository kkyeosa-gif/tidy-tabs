Chosen opening: Total the units with `=SUMIFS(Sales!$D$2:$D$61,Sales!$C$2:$C$61,$A2)`, take revenue after discounts with `=ROUND(D2*B2-SUMIFS(Sales!$E$2:$E$61,Sales!$C$2:$C$61,$A2),2)`, and profit with `=E2-F2`, where F2 is the cost `=ROUND(D2*C2,2)`. ## 2. 2026-11-08-team-02-state-name-to-abbreviation-lookup-index-match-excel-google-sheets
Title candidates: A. How to Look Up a State Abbreviation From the Full State Name in Excel and Google Sheets (chosen). B. Texas to TX Formula in Google Sheets (drops Excel, covers one state). C. How to Convert State Names to Codes in Excel (drops Sheets, "codes" is vaguer than abbreviation).
Chosen opening: Look the code up with `=INDEX(States!$B$2:$B$52,MATCH(D2,States!$A$2:$A$52,0))` after cleaning the typed text in D2 with `=TRIM(SUBSTITUTE(C2,UNICHAR(160)," "))`. The typed text " ohio " returns OH, and a name that is not on the list, such as "Calif.", returns Check instead of a blank.

## 4. 2026-11-08-team-04-last-business-day-of-month-eomonth-workday-excel-google-sheets
Title candidates: A. How to Find the Last Business Day of Each Month in Excel and Google Sheets (chosen). B. Last Working Day of Month Formula in Google Sheets (drops Excel). C. How to Get the Month End Invoice Date on a Weekday in Excel (drops Sheets, wordy).
Chosen opening: Get the month end with `=EOMONTH(A6,0)`, then step back to the last business day with `=WORKDAY(B6+1,-1,Holidays!$B$2:$B$21)`, where the Holidays range is your own list of closed days. December 2026 ends on Thursday 12/31/2026, which is on the sample list, so the last business day is Wednesday 12/30/2026 (the closed days are fictional, not a legal list).

## 5. 2026-11-08-team-05-date-you-hit-sales-goal-cumulative-total-match-excel-google-sheets
Title candidates: A. How to Find the Date You Hit a Sales Goal in Excel and Google Sheets (chosen). B. First Date a Running Total Passes a Target in Google Sheets (drops Excel, abstract). C. When Did Sales Reach $10,000 in Excel (drops Sheets, hard codes the amount).
Chosen opening: Add a running total with `=SUM($B$4:B4)`, mark the first row that reaches the goal with `=IF(C4>=$B$1,"Yes","")`, and return its date with `=IFERROR(INDEX($A$4:$A$369,MATCH("Yes",$D$4:$D$369,0)),"Not yet")`. With a $10,000.00 goal, the sample total first passes it on 09/12/2026, day 12, at $10,612.00 (fictional sales).
