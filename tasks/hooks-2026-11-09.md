## 1. 2026-11-09-team-01-convert-text-hours-minutes-1h-30m-to-minutes-find-mid-excel-google-sheets
Title candidates: A. How to Convert Text Like 2h 30m Into Minutes in Excel and Google Sheets (chosen, uses the exact thing people type and both apps). B. Text Hours and Minutes to Decimal in Google Sheets (drops Excel, vaguer). C. How to Turn 1h 15m Into Minutes in Excel (drops Sheets).
Chosen opening: Strip the spaces and lowercase the entry with `=LOWER(SUBSTITUTE(TRIM(B4)," ",""))`, take the hours before the h with `=IF(ISNUMBER(FIND("h",C4)),VALUE(LEFT(C4,FIND("h",C4)-1)),0)`, and get total minutes with `=ROUND(D4*60+E4,0)`. The entry "2h 30m" becomes 150 minutes, and "45m" becomes 45 (E4 reads the number before the m; the post shows it).

## 2. 2026-11-09-team-02-fiscal-year-quarter-from-date-custom-start-month-excel-google-sheets
Title candidates: A. How to Get the Fiscal Year and Quarter From a Date in Excel and Google Sheets (chosen). B. Fiscal Quarter Formula for a July Start in Google Sheets (drops Excel, hard codes July). C. How to Label Dates as Q1 to Q4 in Excel (drops Sheets, assumes calendar year).
Chosen opening: Put the first month of your year in B1 (7 for July), then get the fiscal year with `=YEAR(A4)+IF(AND($B$1>1,MONTH(A4)>=$B$1),1,0)` and the quarter with `=INT(MOD(MONTH(A4)-$B$1,12)/3)+1`. With a July start, 11/09/2026 is FY2027 Q2 (the year is named for the calendar year it ends in; this labels dates and is not tax advice).

## 3. 2026-11-09-team-03-running-total-that-restarts-each-month-sumifs-excel-google-sheets
Title candidates: A. How to Make a Running Total That Restarts Each Month in Excel and Google Sheets (chosen). B. Month to Date Column in Google Sheets (drops Excel, jargon). C. Cumulative Sum by Month in Excel (drops Sheets, loses "restarts").
Chosen opening: In a date-sorted list, use `=SUMIFS($C$4:C4,$A$4:A4,">="&DATE(YEAR(A4),MONTH(A4),1))` so each row adds up only the amounts from the 1st of its own month down to that row. In the sample, the 11/18/2026 expense shows $407.25 for November, not the $676.75 a plain running total would show (made-up expenses).

## 4. 2026-11-09-team-04-alternate-row-colors-by-group-order-number-conditional-formatting-excel-google-sheets
Title candidates: A. How to Shade Alternating Groups of Rows by Order Number in Excel and Google Sheets (chosen). B. Banded Rows by Value Change in Google Sheets (drops Excel, abstract). C. Alternate Row Colors by Group in Excel (drops Sheets, no order example).
Chosen opening: Number the groups in a helper column with `=IF(A5="","",IF(A5=A4,D4,MAX($D$4:D4)+1))` (D4 holds `=IF(A4="","",1)`, which shows 1; the plain `=IF(A5=A4,D4,D4+1)` also works but numbers the empty rows below the data), then add a conditional formatting rule on the data range with the custom formula `=MOD($D4,2)=0`. Every line of an order gets the same shade and the shade flips when the order number changes, which plain banding with `=MOD(ROW(),2)=0` cannot do. The list must be sorted by order number.

## 5. 2026-11-09-team-05-compare-old-and-new-price-lists-change-percent-index-match-excel-google-sheets
Title candidates: A. How to Compare Two Price Lists and See What Changed in Excel and Google Sheets (chosen). B. Old Price vs New Price in Google Sheets (drops Excel, vague). C. How to Use VLOOKUP to Compare Prices in Excel (drops Sheets, names a function the file does not use).
Chosen opening: For each SKU on the new list, pull the old price with `=IFERROR(INDEX(Old!$B$2:$B$41,MATCH(A4,Old!$C$2:$C$41,0)),"")` (column C of the Old tab holds each SKU after TRIM), then show the change with `=ROUND(B4-C4,2)` and the percent with `=D4/C4` (the file wraps it as `=IF(D4="","",IF(C4=0,"",D4/C4))` so an old price of 0 stays blank). In the sample, CND-8 went from $18.00 to $19.50, up $1.50 or 8.3%, and a SKU with no old price is labeled New (made-up prices).
