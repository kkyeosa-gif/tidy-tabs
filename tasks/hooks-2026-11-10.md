## 1. 2026-11-10-team-01-look-up-most-recent-price-paid-last-match-lookup-excel-google-sheets
Title candidates: A. How to Look Up the Most Recent Price Paid for an Item in Excel and Google Sheets (chosen, the thing people want and both apps). B. VLOOKUP Last Match Instead of First in Google Sheets (drops Excel, names a function the file does not use). C. Get the Last Purchase Price for an Item in Excel (drops Sheets).
Chosen opening: Return the price from the last row that matches an item with `=LOOKUP(2,1/((Purchases!$D$4:$D$203=TRIM(A4))*ISNUMBER(Purchases!$C$4:$C$203)),Purchases!$C$4:$C$203)`. In the sample log, soy wax was bought three times and the formula returns $61.00, the cost on its last row (made-up prices). The last row is not always the newest date, so the template also shows the newest date for the item and flags a mismatch.

## 2. 2026-11-10-team-02-round-order-quantity-up-to-case-size-minimum-order-roundup-excel-google-sheets
Title candidates: A. How to Round an Order Quantity Up to a Case Size and Minimum Order in Excel and Google Sheets (chosen). B. Round Up to a Multiple of 12 in Google Sheets (drops Excel, hard codes 12). C. Minimum Order Quantity Formula in Excel (drops Sheets, ignores the case size).
Chosen opening: Get the number of cases with `=ROUNDUP(MAX(B4,D4)/C4,0)`, where B4 is the units you need, D4 is the supplier's minimum order and C4 is the case size, then multiply by the case size with `=E4*C4`. Needing 130 mailers with a case of 25 and a minimum of 100 gives 6 cases, or 150 units (made-up supplier terms). The file wraps the cases formula so a need of 0 orders nothing.

## 3. 2026-11-10-team-03-count-orders-with-exact-tag-comma-separated-cell-find-sumproduct-excel-google-sheets
Title candidates: A. How to Count Orders With an Exact Tag in a Comma Separated Cell in Excel and Google Sheets (chosen). B. COUNTIF Whole Word Match in Google Sheets (drops Excel, the file does not use whole word COUNTIF). C. Count a Tag in a List in One Cell in Excel (drops Sheets, vague).
Chosen opening: Put a comma at both ends of the cleaned tags (D4 holds `=","&SUBSTITUTE(SUBSTITUTE(LOWER(TRIM(B4)),", ",",")," ,",",")&","`, which the file guards for blank cells), then count a tag with `=SUMPRODUCT(--ISNUMBER(FIND(","&LOWER(TRIM(F4))&",",$D$4:$D$203)))`. In the sample, gift finds 4 orders, while `=COUNTIF(B4:B203,"*gift*")` finds 7 because it also counts gifted and gift wrap (made-up orders).

## 4. 2026-11-10-team-04-convert-cents-to-dollars-payments-export-round-divide-100-excel-google-sheets
Title candidates: A. How to Convert Cents to Dollars in a Payments Export in Excel and Google Sheets (chosen). B. Amount in Cents to Dollars in Google Sheets (drops Excel). C. How to Divide by 100 in Excel (drops Sheets, loses the cents idea).
Chosen opening: Divide the amount in cents by 100 and round to 2 places with `=ROUND(C4/100,2)`. An amount of 1850 becomes $18.50, and a refund of -2400 becomes -$24.00 (made-up payments). To go back from dollars, use `=ROUND(J4*100,0)`, because 19.99*100 can come out as 1998.9999999999998 without the ROUND.

## 5. 2026-11-10-team-05-group-dates-into-14-day-billing-periods-int-excel-google-sheets
Title candidates: A. How to Group Dates Into 14-Day Billing Periods in Excel and Google Sheets (chosen). B. Biweekly Period Number From a Date in Google Sheets (drops Excel, jargon). C. Date to Period Number in Excel (drops Sheets, drops the 14 days).
Chosen opening: Number each date's period with `=INT((A5-$B$1)/$B$2)+1`, where B1 is the date the first period starts and B2 is the number of days in a period. With an 11/02/2026 start and 14 days, 11/15/2026 is period 1 and 11/16/2026 is period 2 (made-up entries). The file wraps the formula so a date before the start shows Before start and a text date shows Check. This groups dates; it does not decide when anyone must bill.
