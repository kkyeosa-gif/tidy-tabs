## 1. 2026-11-11-team-01-shorten-title-to-character-limit-whole-word-left-find-substitute-excel-google-sheets
Title candidates: A. How to Shorten a Product Title to a Character Limit Without Cutting a Word in Excel and Google Sheets (chosen, the job people search for and both apps). B. Truncate Text at a Word Boundary in Google Sheets (drops Excel, jargon). C. Cut Off Text at the Last Space in Excel (drops Sheets, loses the limit in a cell).
Chosen opening: Keep whole words under a limit by cutting the title at the last space inside the first 141 characters, with `=LEFT(TRIM(A4),FIND("~",SUBSTITUTE(LEFT(TRIM(A4),$B$1+1)," ","~",LEN(LEFT(TRIM(A4),$B$1+1))-LEN(SUBSTITUTE(LEFT(TRIM(A4),$B$1+1)," ",""))))-1)`, where B1 holds the limit. A 150 character sample title with a limit of 140 comes out at 138 characters, ending on a whole word (made-up titles). The file wraps this so a title that already fits is copied as is and a title with no space is flagged.

## 2. 2026-11-11-team-02-check-zip-code-format-5-digit-zip-plus-4-len-isnumber-excel-google-sheets
Title candidates: A. How to Check ZIP Codes for the Right Format (5 Digits or ZIP+4) in Excel and Google Sheets (chosen). B. Validate ZIP Code Format in Google Sheets (drops Excel, "validate" suggests data validation rules the file does not use). C. Flag ZIP Codes Missing a Leading Zero in Excel (drops Sheets, covers only one of the checks).
Chosen opening: A ZIP is 5 digits when `=AND(LEN(C4)=5,SUMPRODUCT(--ISNUMBER(FIND(MID(C4,{1,2,3,4,5},1),"0123456789")))=5)` is TRUE, where C4 is `=TRIM(B4)`. In the sample, 02134 passes, 2134 shows Missing leading zero with the fix 02134, and 12O45 (letter O) shows Check (made-up customers). This checks the shape only; it does not confirm that a ZIP exists.

## 3. 2026-11-11-team-03-split-address-with-line-breaks-into-columns-char-10-mid-excel-google-sheets
Title candidates: A. How to Split an Address With Line Breaks Into Separate Columns in Excel and Google Sheets (chosen). B. Split Text by New Line in Google Sheets (drops Excel, and the file does not use SPLIT). C. Separate Text on CHAR(10) in Excel (drops Sheets, jargon in the title).
Chosen opening: Pull line 1 out of a cell that holds line breaks with `=TRIM(CLEAN(MID(SUBSTITUTE(A4,CHAR(10),REPT(" ",LEN(A4))),0*LEN(A4)+1,LEN(A4))))`, then change the 0 to 1, 2 and 3 for lines 2 to 4. A 3 line sample block gives the name, the street and Austin, TX 78701, with column 4 left blank (made-up address). The file wraps each formula so an empty row stays blank, and it works without TEXTSPLIT.

## 4. 2026-11-11-team-04-month-name-to-month-number-match-left-excel-google-sheets
Title candidates: A. How to Turn Month Names Into Month Numbers in Excel and Google Sheets (chosen). B. Convert Jan Feb Mar to 1 2 3 in Google Sheets (drops Excel, reads like a puzzle). C. Month Text to Month Number Formula in Excel (drops Sheets).
Chosen opening: Turn a month name into its number with `=MATCH(LOWER(LEFT(TRIM(A4),3)),Months!$B$4:$B$15,0)`, where Months!B4:B15 lists jan to dec in lower case. Jan, january, JANUARY and "  Jan " all give 1, and Sept. and September give 9 (made-up sales). Only the first three letters are read, so Marc also gives 3; the file returns Check for names it cannot match, such as Ju.

## 5. 2026-11-11-team-05-average-last-n-entries-growing-list-index-count-excel-google-sheets
Title candidates: A. How to Average the Last 5 Entries in a Growing List in Excel and Google Sheets (chosen). B. Average the Last N Values in Google Sheets (drops Excel, N alone is vague). C. Moving Average of the Latest Entries in Excel (drops Sheets, "moving average" suggests a forecast).
Chosen opening: Average the latest N entries with `=AVERAGE(INDEX(B4:B203,MAX(1,F5-3-$B$1+1)):INDEX(B4:B203,F5-3))`, where B1 holds N and F5 holds the row of the last number (`=LOOKUP(2,1/ISNUMBER(B4:B203),ROW(B4:B203))`). With 14 made-up weekly sales and N = 5, it averages entries 10 to 14 and shows $1,632.93, next to $1,439.44 for all 14. The file writes the 3 as ROW($B$3) and adds a Check when N is not a whole number of 1 or more. It describes past numbers; it does not forecast.
