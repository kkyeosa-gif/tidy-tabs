Opening candidates:
- (chosen) Convert a Unix timestamp in B4 to a date and time with `=B4/86400+DATE(1970,1,1)` and format the cell as mm/dd/yyyy h:mm AM/PM; that gives UTC. For US Eastern standard time use `=(B4-5*3600)/86400+DATE(1970,1,1)`, and for a 13-digit timestamp in milliseconds divide by 86400000 instead. In the made-up sample, 1793630100 becomes 11/02/2026 9:35 AM Eastern. Why: formula, format, offset and milliseconds in three sentences.
- A Unix timestamp is the number of seconds since 01/01/1970 UTC, so dividing by 86,400 (seconds in a day) and adding the date 01/01/1970 turns it into a spreadsheet date. Why: explains first, formula second.
- Payment and app exports often list times as a long number such as 1793630100; one formula turns that into a date you can sort and total by day. Why: situation first.
