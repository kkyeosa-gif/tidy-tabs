Final: B. How to Calculate Order Processing Time in Excel and Google Sheets (closest to what sellers type; "the 90th percentile, rounded up" goes in the opening and Steps).
Claims note: processing time here is order date to ship date in business days (NETWORKDAYS minus 1, the reader's holidays skipped); carrier transit is not included. A weekend order shipped Monday counts 0 (confirmed in the template: Saturday 11/21/2026 to Monday 11/23/2026 gives 0). The built cell is `=MAX(0,NETWORKDAYS(INT(B5),INT(C5),Holidays)-1)` inside a Status guard: INT drops a time of day and MAX keeps a Saturday order shipped Sunday at 0 instead of -1. PERCENTILE is the inclusive kind (same as PERCENTILE.INC; link both Microsoft pages), not PERCENTILE.EXC. No claim about Etsy, Shopify or carrier rules; the reader sets the number in their own shop. The past does not guarantee December. Link ship-date-daily-cutoff-time-workday, on-time-shipping-rate-promised-vs-shipped-date and median-order-value-vs-average.
Opening candidates:
- (chosen) Count the business days from order to shipment with `=NETWORKDAYS(B5,C5,Holidays)-1`, then promise the 90th percentile rounded up to a whole day: `=ROUNDUP(PERCENTILE(Orders!$D$5:$D$504,0.9),0)`. In the made-up shop's 68 shipped fall orders the 90th percentile is 4.3, so the promise is 5 business days, which 65 of the 68 orders met. Why: the two formulas up front for the maker setting a holiday processing time, then how many past orders the promise covers.
- The average is the wrong number to promise: the made-up shop's orders averaged 2.72 business days from order to shipment, but only 67.65% shipped within 3 days, while `=ROUNDUP(PERCENTILE(Orders!$D$5:$D$504,0.9),0)` gives 5 days and covers 95.59%. Why: leads with the average-vs-promise gap for the searcher who was going to type their average into the listing.
- Make a table of 0 to 10 days with `=COUNTIF(Orders!$D$5:$D$504,"<="&D6)` beside each, divided by the shipped count, and you can see what each processing time would have covered: in the made-up shop, 3 days covered 67.65% of orders, 4 days 89.71% and 5 days 95.59%. Why: leads with the coverage table for the seller choosing between two numbers, the formula in the same sentence.

## 5. 2026-11-28-team-05-find-recurring-charges-and-subscriptions-in-bank-export-distinct-months-countifs-sumif-excel-google-sheets

How to Find Subscriptions in a Bank Statement in Excel and Google Sheets (matches "identify subscriptions from bank statement spreadsheet"). C. Shortlist: A, B, C.
Final: A. How to Find Recurring Charges and Subscriptions in a Bank Export in Excel and Google Sheets (covers both search wordings; "card export" goes in the Steps).
Claims note: "recurring" means charged in at least the typed number of months (3 in the sample); a monthly plan that renewed once a year shows as One-time and the post says to check those lines. COUNTIF and SUMIF treat `*`, `?` and `~` as wildcards, so the keys are escaped (link Microsoft's wildcard page); the 7-vs-3 count was confirmed on the built sample (unescaped COUNTIF 7 charges and SUMIF 6 months, escaped 3 and 2; LibreOffice only). COUNTIF matching ignores case. No advice on what to cancel or deduct. Merchant names may need cleaning first (link categorize-expenses-by-keyword-search-lookup). Link subscription-renewal-tracker and count-unique-values.
Opening candidates:
- (chosen) Flag the first charge of each merchant in each month with `=IF(COUNTIFS($B$5:B5,D5,$E$5:E5,E5)=1,1,0)`, then add those flags per merchant with SUMIF: a merchant that charged in 3 or more different months is recurring. In the made-up card export, 4 of 8 merchants come out as subscriptions, including one whose price went from $24.00 to $27.00 and one that stopped after 08/08/2026. Why: the distinct-month method up front for the owner reviewing a year of charges, then what the review turned up.
- If a merchant name contains an asterisk, like "SQ *BAKERY", COUNTIF reads it as a wildcard and also counts "SQ *MAIN ST BAKERY": in the made-up export that turned 3 charges in 2 months into 7 charges in 6 months. Escape it first with `=SUBSTITUTE(SUBSTITUTE(SUBSTITUTE(B5,"~","~~"),"*","~*"),"?","~?")`. Why: leads with the wildcard bug for the searcher whose subscription counts are too high, the escape formula right after.
- Count months, not charges: the made-up OFFICE MART charged 5 times but in only 4 months with 5 different amounts, so it shows as a Repeat vendor, while Cloudbox Storage charged $9.99 in all 12 months and shows as a Subscription at $119.88 for the year. Why: leads with the months-vs-charges idea for the searcher whose pivot table counts every charge.
